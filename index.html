The growth of a country’s average living standard may hide the fact that lower-income people are not benefiting at the same pace

A visual story about why average income can hide ordinary living standards.

The world is getting richer, but wealth growth is not always shared equally. In many countries, high-income groups raise the national average level, while ordinary people may experience a completely different reality. This webpage demonstrates this fact by comparing mean income, median income, and income growth among the bottom 40%.

The big question

The world is richer than before, why does the gap between average living standards and ordinary people's lives still remain?

The world is getting richer — on average

This map shows that it is true that many countries are becoming richer, but economic levels are still unequal between countries

Average vs. typical person: the ordinary-life gap

The mean is easily influenced by high-income groups, while the median is closer to the living standard of a typical ordinary person. This shows that the national figures may only look good on paper

Is growth reaching the bottom 40%?

This chart shows that the benefits received by low-income groups are unequal across different countries. A higher green bar means that the bottom groups are also benefiting, while a higher blue bar means that the bottom groups have not benefited at the same pace as national economic growth

Key takeaways

  • Economic growth at the national level can hide unequal living standards between people.
  • Growth in average consumption does not necessarily mean that ordinary people’s consumption has also grown.
  • Growth in average consumption does not necessarily mean that the lower-income groups have also benefited.
